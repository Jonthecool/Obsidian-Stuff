# LR — Timing Strategy

> Timing problems are comprehension problems in disguise. But you still need a system for when to move, when to skip, and when to cut losses.
> Back to [[LSAT Prep Hub]]

---

## The Numbers

- **35 minutes** per LR section
- **24–26 questions** per section
- **~1 minute 20 seconds** per question on average

That average is misleading. Questions are not equal. Use it as a warning signal, not a goal.

---

## The Tiered Pacing System

### Tier 1 — Move Fast (under 1:30)
Questions 1–10 are generally easier. You should be through them in 12–14 minutes.
- If you hit 2 minutes on an early question, something is wrong. Flag it, pick your best guess, move.
- Never spend 3+ minutes on any single question.

### Tier 2 — Steady (1:30–2:30)
Questions 11–20 get harder. Budget ~2 minutes each.
- If you're confident after 90 seconds, move. Don't second-guess clean reads.
- If you're still stuck at 2 minutes, make a decision and go.

### Tier 3 — Triage (last 5–6 questions)
Questions 20–25 are the hardest. These are designed to eat time.
- If you're behind on time entering this zone, skip the hardest-looking ones first.
- A strategic guess and 90 seconds saved on Q23 is worth more than the "right" answer on Q23 and no time for Q24–25.

---

## The Skip Rule

Skip a question when:
- You've read it twice and still don't know what it's asking
- You've been on it for 2+ minutes with no clear path
- It's a question type you consistently miss (no point burning time on your weakest type)

**How to skip:** Bubble a letter immediately, circle the question number, move on. Come back in whatever time remains.

Never leave a question blank. Always have a bubble filled.

---

## The 2-Minute Hard Stop

If you hit 2 minutes on any question, make a call and move. No exceptions.

Two minutes of uncertainty on one question = 90 seconds of clear thinking on the next one sacrificed. The math doesn't work in your favor.

## The Root Cause Reminder

**Timing issues are not speed issues.** You are not too slow. You are spending too long on questions where you don't have a clear framework for what you're looking for.

The fix:
1. Know the question type immediately from the stem
2. Know what you're hunting before you read choices
3. Eliminate by logic, not by feel

Speed follows understanding. Drill comprehension. Timing corrects itself.
