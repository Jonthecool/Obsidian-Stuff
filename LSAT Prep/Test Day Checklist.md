## Remote Option

You can take the LSAT from home. Requirements:
- [ ] Stable internet connection
- [ ] Quiet, private room
- [ ] Working webcam and microphone
- [ ] Government-issued photo ID
- [ ] ProctorU software installed and tested
- [ ] No dual monitors, secondary screens, or phones nearby
> Check [LSAC Remote LSAT Checklist](https://www.lsac.org/lsat/taking-lsat/remote-checklist) for full requirements

---

## Week Before Test Day

- [ ] Confirm test date, time, and location in your LSAC account
- [ ] Do a light review of key strategies — no heavy drilling
- [ ] Sleep consistently — at least 8 hours each night
- [ ] Know your commute to the test center (or tech setup if remote)
- [ ] Prepare your admissions ticket (available in LSAC account)
- [ ] Prepare valid government-issued photo ID

---

## What to Bring (In-Person)

- [ ] LSAC admissions ticket (printed or on phone)
- [ ] Valid government-issued photo ID (exact name must match registration)
- [ ] Pencils (if allowed — check current LSAC policy)
- [ ] Water and a snack for the break
- [ ] Earplugs (check if permitted)
- [ ] Layered clothing (test centers can be cold)

---

## Morning of the Test

- [ ] Eat a real breakfast
- [ ] Arrive at the test center 30 min early (or log in to remote session 15 min early)
- [ ] No cramming — you're done studying
- [ ] Trust your prep

---

## After the Test

- [ ] Score preview available before official release — decide whether to keep or cancel
- [ ] Official scores released **December 2, 2026**
- [ ] Law school application deadlines typically Jan–Feb — you'll be right on time
