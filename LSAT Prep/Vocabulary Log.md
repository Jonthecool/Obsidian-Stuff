## ARGUMENT & REASONING VERBS ★★★
*(The action words LSAT uses to describe what arguments do)*

| Word | Plain Definition | LSAT-Context Usage |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **infer** ★ | to draw a conclusion from evidence, even if not directly stated | "From the data, we can infer that urban birds adapt faster." |
| **imply** ★ | to suggest something without stating it directly | "The author implies, without stating, that the policy will fail." |
| **contend** | to argue or claim | "Critics contend that the proposed regulation is unnecessary." |
| **assert** | to state confidently, without necessarily proving it | "The politician asserted that crime had fallen, citing no data." |
| **maintain** ★ | to continue to hold a position, especially when challenged | "The scientist maintained her position despite new objections." |
| **aver** | to formally declare something is true | "The defense avered that the defendant was elsewhere that night." |
| **posit** | to put forward as a starting assumption or claim | "The argument posits that all humans act in self-interest." |
| **postulate** | to assume something as a starting point for reasoning | "The theory postulates that language shapes thought." |
| **surmise** | to guess or infer with limited evidence | "We can only surmise the author's motivation from the text." |
| **conjecture** | a conclusion based on incomplete information | "The claim is mere conjecture — no data supports it." |
| **speculate** | to form a theory without firm evidence | "The passage speculates about ancient migration routes." |
| **deduce** | to reach a conclusion by logical reasoning from general principles | "From the universal rule, we deduce the specific case." |
| **extrapolate** ★ | to extend a known trend or finding beyond the data | "Extrapolating from city data to rural areas is the argument's flaw." |
| **concede** | to admit something is true, even if it weakens your point | "The author concedes that exceptions exist but argues they're rare." |
| **refute** ★ | to prove something is wrong | "The new fossil evidence refutes the prevailing migration theory." |
| **negate** ★ | to make something invalid or null | "One counterexample negates a universal claim entirely." |
| **invalidate** | to make an argument or claim no longer valid | "The contaminated sample invalidated the experiment's results." |
| **nullify** | to cancel out the effect or validity of something | "The appeals court nullified the lower court's decision." |
| **undermine** ★ | to weaken without directly destroying | "The new data undermines the core assumption of the argument." |
| **bolster** | to strengthen or support | "More field studies would bolster the author's central claim." |
| **substantiate** | to provide evidence that confirms a claim | "The hypothesis was never substantiated by controlled experiments." |
| **corroborate** | to confirm or support with additional evidence | "A second independent study corroborated the initial findings." |
| **validate** | to confirm correctness or legitimacy | "The experiment validated the theoretical model." |
| **elucidate** | to make something clear; to explain | "The author elucidates the mechanism by which bacteria develop resistance." |
| **delineate** | to describe or indicate precisely | "The article delineates three distinct stages of the process." |
| **circumvent** | to find a way around a rule or obstacle | "The company circumvented regulations by rebranding the product." |
| **subvert** | to undermine the authority or integrity of something | "The new law subverts the original intent of the constitution." |
| **supplant** | to replace and push aside | "Digital media has largely supplanted print journalism." |
| **supersede** | to replace in authority or relevance | "The new treaty supersedes all prior bilateral agreements." |
| **preclude** | to prevent something from happening | "The new evidence precludes the defendant's earlier explanation." |
| **forestall** | to prevent by taking action in advance | "Early intervention forestalls the development of chronic illness." |
| **impede** | to delay or block | "Bureaucratic rules impede scientific innovation." |
| **engender** | to cause or give rise to | "Inequality engenders resentment that destabilizes society." |
| **precipitate** | to cause something to happen suddenly | "The scandal precipitated the CEO's resignation." |
| **catalyze** | to speed up or trigger a process | "The invention catalyzed rapid industrial growth." |
| **facilitate** ★ | to make something easier or possible | "Good policy facilitates economic mobility." |
| **exacerbate** | to make a problem worse | "Cutting funding exacerbates inequality in school outcomes." |
| **mitigate** | to reduce the severity of something | "The vaccine mitigated the severity of the illness." |
| **ameliorate** | to make something better | "The program ameliorated conditions in underserved communities." |
| **alleviate** | to partially relieve without solving | "The drug alleviates symptoms without curing the disease." |
| **proliferate** | to increase rapidly in number | "Misinformation proliferates on unmoderated platforms." |
| **permeate** | to spread through all parts of something | "Skepticism permeated the scientific community's response." |
| **disseminate** | to spread information widely | "The study's results were disseminated through peer-reviewed journals." |
| **propagate** | to spread or promote widely | "The myth was propagated by early historians and never corrected." |
| **promulgate** | to make a law or rule officially known | "The agency promulgated new safety standards in 2022." |

## Vocabulary by Category

### Law & Legal (RC Law passages)
- jurisprudence, precedent, promulgate, enumerate, statute, tort, adjudicate, litigate

### Science & Biology
- corroborate, refute, hypothesis, empirical, extrapolate, infer, taxonomy

### Argument Structure (LR)
- premise, contend, assert, conclude, infer, rebut, concede, qualify

### Dense/Abstract (Social Science)
- paradigm, normative, epistemology, ontological, hegemony, posit
