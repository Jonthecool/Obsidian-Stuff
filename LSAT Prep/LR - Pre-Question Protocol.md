# LR — Pre-Question Protocol

> Run this checklist mentally before touching any answer choice. The goal: never pick an answer before you know what you're looking for.
> Back to [[LSAT Prep Hub]]

---

## The 4-Step Protocol

### Step 1 — Read the Question Stem FIRST
Before reading the stimulus, read the question stem.

Ask: **What is this question asking me to do?**

Examples:
- "Which one of the following is an assumption the argument requires?" → Necessary Assumption
- "The argument is most vulnerable to criticism on the grounds that..." → Flaw
- "Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the argument?" → Strengthen

> **Why first?** The stem tells you what to hunt for in the stimulus. Reading the stimulus without knowing the question type is like walking into a room without knowing what you're looking for.

---

### Step 2 — Identify the Conclusion and Premises
While reading the stimulus, locate:

- **Conclusion** — the claim the author is trying to prove (look for: therefore, thus, so, hence, clearly, must be, it follows that)
- **Premises** — the reasons offered as support (look for: because, since, given that, after all)

State them simply in your own words:

> "The conclusion is: ___. The support is: ___."

If you can't identify the conclusion in 30 seconds, you're in trouble. Flag it and move on if needed.

---

### Step 3 — Spot the Gap (Before Reading Choices)
Ask: **What's the jump between the premises and the conclusion?**

Every LR argument has a gap — something assumed but never stated. Your job is different depending on question type:

| Question Type | What to Find Before Choices |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| Flaw | What logical error bridges premise → conclusion? |
| Necessary Assumption | What MUST be true for the conclusion to hold? |
| Sufficient Assumption | What would GUARANTEE the conclusion? |
| Strengthen | What makes the gap smaller? |
| Weaken | What makes the gap bigger or exposes it? |
| Main Point | What is the conclusion — stated cleanly? |
| Inference/MBT | What must be true based only on what's stated? |

---

### Step 4 — Pre-Phrase an Answer
Before reading choices, try to articulate what the right answer should look like.

It doesn't need to be exact. A rough sketch is enough:
- For Flaw: "The author assumes that just because X, Y must follow — but that doesn't work because..."
- For NA: "The argument collapses if it's not true that..."
- For Weaken: "The argument breaks if we find out that..."

When your pre-phrase matches an answer choice, that's your answer. When nothing matches, eliminate by logic — not by feel.

---

## The Anti-Traps (What to Avoid)

❌ **Don't read choices before forming your own view.** Answer choices are designed to mislead. They exploit your uncertainty.

❌ **Don't pick an answer because it "sounds smart" or "feels right."** Every wrong answer choice sounds reasonable. That's the trap.

❌ **Don't skip Step 1.** If you don't know the question type, you don't know what you're evaluating.

❌ **Don't change your answer based on vibes.** If you changed a right answer to a wrong one, note it in [[Daily Question Log]].

---

## Quick Reference (Pocket Version)

```
1. STEM first — what am I doing?
2. Find CONCLUSION + PREMISES
3. Identify the GAP
4. Pre-phrase the ANSWER
Then — and only then — read choices.
```
