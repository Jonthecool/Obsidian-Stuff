# RC — Question Types

> RC questions are predictable. Recognize the type immediately and know your attack.
> Back to [[LSAT Prep Hub]]

---

## The Core RC Question Types

### 1. Main Point / Primary Purpose
**What it asks:** What is the passage *overall* trying to do?
**Attack:** Use your passage map. The correct answer covers the *whole* passage — not just one section.
**Common traps:** Answers that are too narrow (describe only one paragraph) or too broad (could apply to any passage on this topic).

---

### 2. Author's Attitude / Tone
**What it asks:** How does the author feel about X?
**Attack:** Go back to the evaluative language you flagged while reading. The correct answer matches the author's *degree* of feeling — not too strong, not too weak.
**Common traps:** Extreme answers ("enthusiastic support") when the author is measured; neutral answers when the author clearly has a view.

---

### 3. Detail / Specific Reference ("According to the passage...")
**What it asks:** What does the passage say about a specific point?
**Attack:** Locate the relevant paragraph (don't rely on memory). Read 2–3 sentences around the reference. The answer will be a close paraphrase — not a word-for-word match.
**Common traps:** Answers that are true but *not stated* in the passage; answers that mix details from different paragraphs.

---

### 4. Inference ("It can be inferred..." / "The author would most likely agree...")
**What it asks:** What must be true given what the passage says?
**Attack:** The answer won't be stated directly — but it must follow necessarily from the text. No outside knowledge.
**Common traps:** Answers that go too far beyond what the passage supports; answers that are possible but not necessary.

---

### 5. Function ("The author mentions X in order to...")
**What it asks:** Why does the author include a specific example, detail, or paragraph?
**Attack:** Ask: what *purpose* does this serve in the argument? Is it evidence? A counterexample? A qualification?
**Common traps:** Answers that describe *what* the example says rather than *why* it's there.

---

### 6. Analogy ("Which of the following is most analogous to...?")
**What it asks:** Find a situation that has the same *logical relationship* or *structure* as something in the passage.
**Attack:** Abstract the relationship in the passage to its core logic, then find the answer that matches that logic — even if the topic is completely different.
**Common traps:** Answers that match the *topic* but not the *structure*.

---

### 7. Strengthen / Weaken (rare in RC)
**What it asks:** Which answer supports or undermines a claim made in the passage?
**Attack:** Same as LR Strengthen/Weaken — identify the specific claim being targeted and find what helps or hurts it.

---

### 8. Meaning of a Word / Phrase ("As used in line X, the word Y most nearly means...")
**What it asks:** What does a specific word or phrase mean *in context*?
**Attack:** Re-read the sentence and surrounding sentences. The answer is context-dependent — don't default to the word's most common meaning.

---

## Quick Reference Table

| Question Type | Where to Look | Key Watch-Out |
|--------------|--------------|--------------|
| Main Point | Whole passage | Too narrow or too broad |
| Author's Attitude | Evaluative language | Wrong degree of feeling |
| Detail | Specific paragraph | True but not stated |
| Inference | Nearby text | Goes too far |
| Function | Surrounding context | What vs. why |
| Analogy | Logical structure | Topic match ≠ structure match |
| Strengthen/Weaken | Specific claim | Correct claim targeted? |
| Word Meaning | Line in context | Don't use dictionary definition |
